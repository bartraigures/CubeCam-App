Support

These are the questions that come up most often. The guide explains every part of the app in more detail.

What does CubeCam need?

An iPhone running iOS 18 or later. Camera Control features need an iPhone with Camera Control, which means an iPhone 16 or later. The focal lengths on offer depend on the iPhone's cameras: 13 mm needs an ultra-wide camera, and 100 and 200 mm need a telephoto camera.

How do I make CubeCam the default camera?

The iPhone doesn't have a default camera setting. Instead, Camera Control, the Lock Screen camera button, the Action button and Control Center each let you choose which camera they open. The guide and CubeCam's own Settings have the steps for each. If CubeCam isn't offered in one of those lists, open CubeCam once with the phone unlocked, restart the iPhone, and look again.

A photograph hasn't appeared in the Photos app

Photographs normally reach the library within a second or two of being taken. Every shot is kept inside CubeCam until it is safely in the library, so nothing is lost when one is late. If CubeCam was closed while a photograph was still saving, it finishes the next time CubeCam opens. Photographs taken with the phone locked are moved into the library the next time CubeCam is opened unlocked. If neither of those applies, Photos access may have been switched off, which is covered next.

CubeCam says Photos access is off

CubeCam can't save to the library, but it keeps your photographs until it can. Open Settings, go to CubeCam, then Photos, and choose Limited Access or Full Access. Add Photos Only allows saving but stops CubeCam's photo viewer working. When CubeCam is next opened, the waiting photographs are saved straight away. Choosing Don't Allow when the photo viewer first asks for access is the usual cause, because it switches off all Photos access, saving included.

iOS asks whether to allow CubeCam to modify a photo

iOS asks this whenever any app saves a change to a photograph that is already in the library, and it appears every time an edit is saved in CubeCam's viewer. The change is saved as a revertible edit, so the photograph can always be put back.

The look of a photograph can't be changed

Changing a look needs Apple's original, which CubeCam keeps when Keep original is switched on in Settings, under Saving. Photographs taken with it off have their look built in, although they can still be adjusted. Photographs taken before the photo viewer was added to CubeCam don't appear in the viewer at all.

Where is the ProRAW file?

With RAW switched on, the ProRAW file is saved untouched as a photograph of its own, next to the graded one. The Photos app marks it with a RAW badge.

Why are CubeCam's files larger than Apple's?

Grain is fine detail at every pixel, and fine detail doesn't compress much. A 24 megapixel photograph from CubeCam is usually between 6 and 8 MB, and a 48 megapixel one considerably more. Turning the grain down or off in Settings, or choosing 12 or 24 megapixels, gives smaller files.

Where has the Settings button gone?

Settings is always somewhere on the camera screen, since it can be moved in the layout editor but not removed. It is also in the menu that opens when you tap the look's name at the top of the screen.

Manual focus and lens lock

Manual focus needs the Focus control on the camera screen, which the Manual layout already includes. Switching between automatic and manual focus, and locking or unlocking a lens, makes the picture blink briefly while the iPhone changes cameras. A locked lens can't focus as close as the iPhone can when it is free to switch to the ultra-wide, so release the lock for very close subjects.

The locked camera

Opened from the Lock Screen, CubeCam works without Face ID, in the same way as Apple's Camera, and can take photographs but can't show the library until the phone is unlocked. Changes to your settings reach the locked camera the next time CubeCam is opened with the phone unlocked.
